I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Speech, Language, and Hearing Sciences at The University of Texas at Austin, advised by Dr. Chang Liu. I am also completing clinical audiology training toward professional certification and have accumulated more than 500 hours of supervised clinical experience.

My research focuses on speech understanding in noise and other adverse listening conditions. I study how cognitive, neural, and social factors contribute to individual differences in speech perception using behavioral and electrophysiological methods.

Email CV Google Scholar LinkedIn

Research Focus

My current work examines how listeners understand speech in challenging environments, including the effects of background noise, working memory, vocabulary knowledge, prior experience, and social contact.

  • Speech perception in noise
  • Individual differences in speech understanding
  • Auditory learning and plasticity
  • Behavioral and electrophysiological methods

Research

Speech Perception in Noise

I study how different types of background noise influence speech understanding and short-term memory.

Individual Differences in Speech Understanding

I examine how working memory, vocabulary knowledge, prior experience, and social contact contribute to variability in speech perception.

Auditory Learning and Electrophysiology

I use behavioral and electrophysiological approaches to investigate auditory learning, plasticity, and neural responses to sound.
